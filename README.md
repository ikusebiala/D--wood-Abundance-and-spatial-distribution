Spatial Distribution and Abundance of Standing Deadwood in Trelease Woods

Project Brief

Background

Deadwood is an important component of forest ecosystems because standing dead trees provide structural habitat and contribute to carbon and nutrient cycling. This project will use an openly available standing deadwood dataset from Trelease Woods to develop a reproducible GIS workflow for mapping deadwood locations, quantifying deadwood abundance and examining how abundance varies spatially.

Spatial Question

How does the abundance and spatial distribution of standing deadwood vary with distance from the road bordering Trelease Woods?

Study Area

The study covers Trelease Woods, Urbana, Illinois, USA, an old growth forest remnant of approximately 24 ha. The study area is located at approximately 40.1297° N, 88.1437° W.

Objectives

1. Map the locations of standing deadwood within Trelease Woods.
2. Quantify standing deadwood abundance using available volume, biomass and tree count information.
3. Examine the spatial variation in standing deadwood abundance.
4. Analyse the relationship between standing deadwood abundance and distance from the road bordering the study area.

Datasets Required

1. Standing Deadwood Census Data: Contains GPS coordinates, volume, biomass and decay class for standing dead trees recorded at Trelease Woods in 2022.
   Source: Illinois Data Bank.
   DOI: 10.13012/B2IDB-5980585_V1
   Link: https://databank.illinois.edu/datasets/IDB-5980585

2. Road Network Data: Used to identify the road bordering the western side of Trelease Woods and calculate distance zones from the road.
   Source: OpenStreetMap.
   Link: https://www.openstreetmap.org/

3. Trelease Woods Study Area Information: Used to define and document the study area and its geographic location.
   Source: ForestGEO, Smithsonian Institution.
   Link: https://forestgeo.si.edu/sites/north-america/trelease-woods
